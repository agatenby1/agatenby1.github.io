Prescribing is the visible fraction, not the whole cost

No government body publishes a yearly figure for total NHS spending on diabetes. The best estimates come from one-off academic cost-of-illness models. They put direct UK NHS costs at £10.7bn in 2021/22, with about 60% going on complications (Hex et al., Diabet Med 2024). The 2010/11 estimate was £9.8bn, about 10% of the NHS budget, projected to reach about 17% by 2035/36 (Hex et al., Diabet Med 2012). These are modelled UK-wide estimates, not Official Statistics. They aren't like-for-like with the England prescribing series above, so they're kept out of the chart.

      What this does not show

      • England only. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland publish prescribing on different bases, and no single UK series exists.
      • Primary-care prescriptions dispensed in the community only. Hospital, prison and private prescriptions are excluded, so private Mounjaro for weight loss is not counted (methodology).
      • List price (Net Ingredient Cost), not what the NHS actually paid. Discounts, dispensing fees and prescription charges are excluded (NHS Digital).
      • Cash terms. Some of the 4.5× rise is general inflation. The share-of-spend bars are a ratio, so inflation doesn't affect them.
      • Drugs are counted by BNF section, not by why they were prescribed. SGLT2 inhibitors for heart failure or CKD, and tirzepatide for weight management, all count as "diabetes".
      • Two publishers, small revisions. Each year before 2016/17 comes from the latest NHS Digital summary that states it. Figures can move slightly between editions. The NHS Digital CSVs have the full split for 2005/06 to 2015/16 and would fill the gaps in the bars.
